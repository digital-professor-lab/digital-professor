What "Good" Looks Like — the PhD Target Case

The interactive method on Number Theory 1, a PhD-level course — precisely the high-level case that motivated this whole line of work.

Advanced graduate courses are exactly where a fixed syllabus is least likely to exist and where a textbook's table of contents overshoots worst (see the summary's §5). An instructor interview sidesteps both: the professor states the real arc and the real prerequisites (here, "passed the quals").

What I gave the model: only a four-line topic list — no syllabus, no textbook:
  • Dedekind domains and ramification
  • Global and local fields
  • Adèles and idèles
  • Tate's thesis (if time permits)
Everything else — module structure, prerequisites, difficulty analysis, assessments, and example questions — the model produced from those four lines plus the intake interview.

A Fully Expanded Topic (§1.6 · Decomposition & Inertia Groups, Frobenius)

Core Concepts
  • Decomposition group $D_P$ and inertia group $I_P$ for a prime $P$ above $p$ in a Galois extension $L/K$
  • The exact sequence $1 \to I_P \to D_P \to \mathrm{Gal}(k(P)/k(p)) \to 1$
  • The Frobenius element / conjugacy class at unramified primes
  • Statement of Chebotarev density (for motivation)
Learning Objectives
  1. Derive the structure of $D_P$ and $I_P$ from the Galois action on primes above $p$.
  2. Compute Frobenius elements in explicit Galois extensions.
  3. Explain how Frobenius conjugacy classes encode splitting behavior.
Why it's historically hard. Students must track three interacting group actions (the full Galois group, $D_P$, $I_P$) simultaneously, and the analogy with the residue field's Galois group is easy to lose.
Mitigation. Use one running example ($\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$) throughout, with explicit subgroup-lattice diagrams at each step.
Example Questions
Foundational Compute $D_P, I_P$ for a split, an inert, and a ramified prime in a quadratic field.Answer: split: $D=I=1$; inert: $D=\mathbb{Z}/2, I=1$; ramified: $D=I=\mathbb{Z}/2$.
Advanced Compute the Frobenius at $p=2$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)/\mathbb{Q}$ and determine the splitting of $2$.Answer: the order of $2$ mod $7$ is $3$, so $f=3$; there are three unramified primes, each with $f=3$.
