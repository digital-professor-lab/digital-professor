Module: Dedekind Domains and Ramification

Module-breakdown method · 7 core sessions (+1 optional) · numerical question bank · pen-and-paper

1 · Intake Settings (stated as assumptions)

  • Session count: 7 core sessions plus an optional Session 8 (Frobenius in cyclotomic fields & quadratic reciprocity) — droppable, nothing earlier depends on it.
  • Question bank: numerical, explicit computations in specific rings/fields; hand-sized numbers (primes below 30).
  • Assumed audience: first-year graduate students who know Galois theory (incl. finite fields), basic commutative algebra, and elementary number theory.

2 · Key Definitions (stated verbatim in lecture)

Dedekind domainAn integral domain that is Noetherian, integrally closed, and of Krull dimension one (every nonzero prime ideal is maximal) — equivalently, every nonzero fractional ideal is invertible.
Ramification index & residue degreeFor $\mathfrak{P}$ above $p$ with $p\mathcal{O}_K = \prod \mathfrak{P}_i^{e_i}$, the exponent $e_i$ is the ramification index and $f_i=[\,\mathcal{O}_K/\mathfrak{P}_i : \mathbb{F}_p\,]$ the residue degree, with $\sum e_i f_i = [K:\mathbb{Q}]$.

3 · Question Bank (samples)

Basic Factor $(2)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and verify the fundamental identity $\sum e_i f_i = n$.Solution: $(2) = (1+i)^2$, so $e=2, f=1, n=2$ and $2\cdot 1 = 2$. (2 ramifies, since $2\mid \operatorname{disc}\mathbb{Q}(i)$.)
Advanced Compute the Frobenius at $p=2$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_7)/\mathbb{Q}$ and determine the splitting of $2$.Solution: the order of $2$ mod $7$ is $3$, so $f=3$; there are three unramified primes above $2$, each of residue degree $3$.
