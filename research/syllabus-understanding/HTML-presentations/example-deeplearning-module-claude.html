Week 1 — Module 1: Neural Networks as Statistical Models

Module-breakdown method · 2 sessions × 75 min · theoretical question bank · pen-and-paper

3 · Key Definitions (stated verbatim in lecture)

Negative log-likelihood (NLL)$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_i \log p_\theta(y_i\mid x_i)$. Minimizing it over $\theta$ is maximum likelihood estimation — every standard loss in this course is an NLL under some noise model.
Canonical linkThe link $g$ for which the linear predictor equals the natural parameter of the exponential family: logit for Bernoulli, log for Poisson, identity for Gaussian.

4 · Measurable Objectives & Difficulty

Each learning objective is paired with a measurable objective — a condition, an observable behavior, a criterion, and where it's measured (here, the Week-2 quiz, in-class checks, and Project 1, since Week 1 has no quiz).

Difficult concept spotlight. Students experience backprop as notational overload — losing track of which Jacobian multiplies which. Mitigation: derive a 2-layer net by hand before the computational-graph formalism, then re-derive via the graph to show it's the same chain rule reorganized.

5 · Question Bank (samples, with solutions)

1.1 · Intermediate Contrast the output-layer gradient of MSE vs. binary cross-entropy on a sigmoid; explain the saturation difference.Solution: BCE with a sigmoid gives the clean $p-y$ gradient; MSE on a sigmoid multiplies by $\sigma'(z)$, which vanishes when the unit saturates — so MSE learns slowly on confident-but-wrong outputs.
1.2 · Advanced Compare the cost of forward-mode vs. reverse-mode differentiation for a scalar loss, and say why reverse mode wins.Solution: reverse mode computes the full gradient of a scalar loss in one backward pass, independent of parameter count; forward mode costs one pass per input direction.
