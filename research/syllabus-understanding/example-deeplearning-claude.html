A Fully Expanded Topic (Topic 1.1 · From GLMs to Neural Networks)

Core Concepts
  • Neural networks as compositions of generalized linear models: each layer is an affine map plus a link-like nonlinearity.
  • Loss functions as negative log-likelihoods under an assumed output-noise model: squared error $\leftrightarrow$ Gaussian, cross-entropy $\leftrightarrow$ Bernoulli/categorical.
  • Final-layer activation as a choice of exponential-family output distribution.
  • Maximum likelihood: $\hat\theta = \arg\max_\theta \sum_i \log p_\theta(y_i \mid x_i)$.
Learning Objectives
  1. Explain why minimizing squared error is equivalent to Gaussian MLE.
  2. Derive the cross-entropy loss from the Bernoulli/categorical log-likelihood.
  3. Apply the GLM-to-network analogy to justify an output activation given a stated noise model.
A difficulty spotlight from the next topic (backpropagation). Students often experience backprop as notational overload — losing track of which Jacobian multiplies which, especially with branching graphs.
Mitigation. Require a from-scratch backprop derivation for a 2-layer net by hand before showing the general computational-graph formalism, then re-derive it via the graph to show it is the same chain rule, reorganized.
Example Questions
Basic Write the negative log-likelihood for $y_i \mid x_i \sim \mathcal{N}(f_\theta(x_i), \sigma^2)$ and show it reduces to squared error up to a constant.Answer: $-\log p = \tfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}(y_i - f_\theta(x_i))^2 + \text{const}$; minimizing over $\theta$ is equivalent to minimizing squared error.
Advanced A student trains a classifier with one-hot targets using squared error. What output-distribution assumption does this impose, and why is it a poor match?Answer: it implicitly assumes Gaussian noise on a categorical target, giving miscalibrated gradients and poor probability estimates near saturation.
